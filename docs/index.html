01 / PROFILE

Theory and simulations across many astrophysical scales.

My work sits where cosmology, high-energy astrophysics, and scientific computing meet.

I combine analytical theory, relativistic flow solutions, hydrodynamic and N-body simulations, Monte Carlo radiative transfer, and statistical analysis to connect physical mechanisms with the formation of stars, haloes, and black holes.

I am especially interested in questions that demand both physical interpretation and careful numerical validation: how primordial gas collapses, how dark matter responds, how photons gain energy in hot plasmas, and how resolution affects the conclusions we draw.

ILLUSTRATIVE THEORY & TIME-DEPENDENT MODEL

Gas inflow, radiative activity, feedback and recovery.

A continuous projected gas-density field shows how the external reservoir grows and is then depleted by feedback. Blue indicates diffuse gas; warmer colours indicate higher projected density.

The accretion disk responds to the external gas supply with a finite viscous delay. Its outer radius decreases as its modelled mass declines, while luminosity, winds and bipolar jets fade. Cooling and gravity permit some bound gas to return, restarting the cycle.

Two complete 16-second cycles are shown in a 32-second MP4, looping automatically. All quantities are normalized and schematic; this animation is not a self-consistent GRMHD simulation.
